Best place to sell high-ticket items: fees on a $500 sale

At $500 the fixed fees stop mattering and the percentage is everything: 20% on Poshmark is $100, 13.6% on eBay is $68 plus 40¢, and 2.9% + 30¢ on your own store is $14.80. High-ticket sellers should treat the platform fee as the main cost of distribution.

What the fee table does not show

Three costs decide the channel more often than the fee does. First, demand: a marketplace brings buyers, an own store does not, and a 10% fee is cheaper than a 30% ad cost per order. Second, returns: a returned order refunds the price, not always the fee, and a marketplace's return rate in high-ticket items is usually higher than a store's. Third, cash timing: marketplaces pay out on a schedule with a reserve, so revenue earned this week may arrive next month, which matters if you are buying inventory.

Questions

What is the cheapest platform for high-ticket items at $500?

On fees alone, Stripe at $14.80 (3.0%) for a $500 sale. Second is Shopify at $15.00 and third Etsy at $47.95.

Does the cheapest platform mean the most profit for high-ticket items?

No. A platform with low fees and no buyers earns nothing. The fee is the cost of distribution — compare it with what you would spend on ads to reach the same buyer from your own store.

How much do fees take on a $500 high-ticket items sale?

Between 3.0% and 20.0% of the price across the platforms listed here — $85.20 of difference per order.
