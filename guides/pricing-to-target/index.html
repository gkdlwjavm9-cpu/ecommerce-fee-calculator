What to charge so you actually keep $20

"Add 10% for fees" fails because fees are not proportional. A platform with a fixed 50¢ per sale needs a different price than one with a 20% commission, and the gap widens as the target grows. Here is the price each platform needs to leave you $10, $20 and $50 per order.

Computed from each platform's full fee stack at US rates checked 11 November 2026, with storefront plans spread over 200 orders. Amazon includes $4.15 FBA fulfilment, so its price includes fulfilment rather than shipping.

The three rules behind the table

1. Percentage platforms need a multiplier, not an add-on. To keep $20 on a 20% commission you charge $25 — 1.25× the target. To keep $50 you charge $62.50. The multiplier is constant; the dollar gap grows.

2. Fixed-fee platforms need a flat add-on. On Shopify with 2.9% + 30¢, keeping $20 needs $20.91; keeping $50 needs $51.81. The fixed 30¢ plus the 2.9% adds up to about $1–2 whatever the price.

3. Mixed stacks need both. Etsy, eBay and Gumroad combine a percentage with a fixed per-order charge, so the required price is a multiplier plus a flat amount. That is why a spreadsheet beats mental arithmetic here.

Check it against your real product cost

The target payout in this table is before the cost of the product and before ads. If your product costs 30% of the sale price and you want $8 of profit after a $6 cost per order, you are really targeting a contribution of about $14 — set the target accordingly and the price rises again.
