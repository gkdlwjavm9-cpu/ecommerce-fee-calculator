Your effective fee rate is not the headline rate

Sellers compare platforms by the number in the pricing table — 6.5%, 10%, 15% — then wonder why the payout is smaller than the arithmetic said. The gap has a name and a size: the effective fee rate is every fee divided by the price, and on a $40 order it can be double the headline.

Etsy is the clearest case: 6.5% is the transaction fee, but processing at 3% + 25¢ and the 20¢ listing fee bring the real cost to about 10.6%. The headline number is not wrong; it is just one of three fees.

Why fixed fees matter more than percentages below $25

A 30¢ card fee is 1.5% of a $20 sale and 6% of a $5 sale. The same is true of a 50¢ platform fee, a 40¢ per-order fee and a 25¢ processing fee: none of them scale with price, so a low-priced catalogue pays a much higher rate for the same platform. That is why the platform with the lowest commission is often not the cheapest for cheap items — compare the $10 and $15 pages before you pick.

How to use the effective rate

  1. Take your real average order value, not your list price.
  2. Add up every fee on that order, including processing and per-order charges.
  3. Divide by the order value. That is your effective rate.
  4. Divide the price by your contribution after product cost to get the break-even ROAS for that channel.

Do that for three channels and the ranking usually changes from the one you expected.
